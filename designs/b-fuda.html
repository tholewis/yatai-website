屋台とはWhat a yatai is

Yatai (屋台) are small food stalls: a counter, a canopy, a lantern and usually one cook. You find them in two places in Japan today: in long rows at festivals and shrine fairs, and every evening in Fukuoka, where about 100 stalls set up on the street. 1

The word has a second meaning. In 2016 UNESCO listed 33 “Yama, Hoko, Yatai” float festivals, and there yatai means a decorated festival float, not a place to eat. 13

お品書きThe menu

25 dishes you will find at stalls, grouped by how they are cooked. Use them as a checklist, or as ideas for your own stall.

鉄

On the iron griddle 鉄板

Cooked on hot iron plates and in iron moulds.

  • たこ焼き

    Takoyaki octopus balls

    Balls of batter with a piece of octopus inside, cooked in a griddle of half-round moulds. The sauce on top only arrived after the war.19

    Osaka · all year

  • お好み焼き

    Okonomiyaki Osaka style

    A batter of flour and dashi mixed with cabbage and pork or squid, then cooked on both sides.21

    Osaka · all year

  • 広島焼き

    Hiroshima okonomiyaki layered style

    Built in layers rather than mixed: a thin crêpe, cabbage, pork, yakisoba noodles and egg.22

    Hiroshima · all year

  • 焼きそば

    Yakisoba fried noodles

    Wheat noodles stir-fried on the plate with meat and vegetables in a Worcester-style sauce. One of the most common festival stalls.23, 24

    Nationwide · festivals

  • いか焼き

    Ikayaki grilled squid

    At festivals, usually a whole squid grilled and brushed with soy. In Osaka the name means chopped squid in a thin batter, pressed flat between two hot iron plates.26

    Nationwide · Osaka style

  • 箸巻き

    Hashimaki chopstick roll

    A thin okonomiyaki rolled around a pair of chopsticks so it can be eaten while walking.27

    Festivals

炭

Grill and fryer 炭火・揚げ

Skewers over the coals and anything that goes into hot oil.

  • 焼き鳥

    Yakitori grilled chicken

    Chicken on skewers, grilled and finished with tare (soy, sugar and mirin) or plain salt.25

    Nationwide · all year

  • 焼きとうもろこし

    Yaki-tōmorokoshi grilled corn

    Corn grilled and brushed with soy sauce. In Sapporo it is called tōkibi and sold from carts in Odori Park from late April to mid-October.28

    Hokkaido · summer

  • フランクフルト

    Furanku sausage on a stick

    A thick sausage on a stick, grilled and finished with ketchup and mustard.30

    Festivals

  • 唐揚げ

    Karaage fried chicken

    Marinated chicken, fried. The Japan Karaage Association traces it to Mikasa Kaikan in Ginza in 1932. Nakatsu in Ōita has around 60 take-out karaage shops.31

    Nationwide · festivals

  • 串カツ

    Kushikatsu fried skewers

    Battered, deep-fried skewers from Shinsekai in Osaka. The rule: dip once in the shared sauce, never twice.32

    Osaka · all year

鍋

From the pot 鍋

The menu of a Fukuoka stall at night: broth, noodles and small plates to share.

  • 博多ラーメン

    Hakata ramen pork-bone ramen

    Tonkotsu broth with thin, straight noodles. Ask for kaedama to get a second serving of noodles in the same soup, a custom said to have started at the Nagahama stalls.10

    Fukuoka · every night

  • おでん

    Oden simmered pot

    Daikon, egg, konnyaku and more, simmered in broth. A winter dish that is now sold all year. Fukuoka stalls add gyoza and beef tendon.33, 6

    Nationwide · winter

  • 明太玉子焼き

    Mentai tamagoyaki roe omelette

    A rolled omelette with spicy pollock roe (mentaiko), made for sharing across the counter.6

    Fukuoka stalls

  • 一口餃子

    Hitokuchi gyoza one-bite dumplings

    Bite-sized gyoza, a Hakata specialty.9

    Fukuoka

甘

Sweet 甘味

Moulded cakes, candied fruit and things on sticks.

  • たい焼き

    Taiyaki fish-shaped cake

    A fish-shaped cake filled with sweet bean paste. Naniwaya Sōhonten in Azabu-Jūban, open since 1909, is credited with the first.34

    Tokyo origin · all year

  • 今川焼き

    Imagawayaki round bean cake

    The round original, first sold near Imagawa Bridge in Edo in the 1770s. Depending on the region it is also called ōbanyaki, kaitenyaki or oyaki.35

    Nationwide · all year

  • みたらし団子

    Mitarashi dango glazed dumplings

    Grilled rice dumplings in a sweet soy glaze. Tradition links them to Shimogamo Shrine in Kyoto.36

    Kyoto · all year

  • りんご飴

    Ringo-ame candy apple

    A whole apple in a shell of hard red candy.37

    Summer festivals

  • チョコバナナ

    Choco banana chocolate banana

    A banana on a stick, dipped in chocolate and covered with sprinkles.38

    Festivals

  • わたあめ

    Wataame cotton candy

    Cotton candy, spun at the stall.24

    Festivals

季

By the season 季節

Food that belongs to one time of year.

  • かき氷

    Kakigōri shaved ice

    Shaved ice with syrup. Sei Shōnagon mentions it in The Pillow Book, about a thousand years ago.39

    Summer

  • 焼き芋

    Yaki-imo stone-roasted sweet potato

    Sweet potato slow-roasted on hot stones and sold from trucks with a sung call.40

    Late autumn · winter

  • 甘酒

    Amazake sweet rice drink

    A sweet drink made from rice koji, under 1% alcohol, served warm at shrines over New Year.41

    New Year

  • じゃがバター

    Jaga-batā butter potato

    A steamed potato split open with a slab of butter, sold next to the corn carts in Sapporo.29

    Hokkaido · summer

福岡の夜A night in Fukuoka

Fukuoka is the one city where yatai open every night. About 100 stalls set up in three areas, each with a counter for about ten people. 1, 11

  • 中洲

    Nakasu

    Along the Naka River. The best-known row of stalls.

  • 天神

    Tenjin

    In the shopping district, close to the stations.

  • 長浜

    Nagahama

    Near the fish market, where the kaedama noodle refill is said to have started.

Sources for the areas: 3, 10

Before you go

Hours
Most open from around 6pm and stay open late. Each stall sets its own hours. 3, 7
Weather
A stall may not open in stormy weather. Check the city's map of open stalls before you go. 7
Payment
Most stalls take cash only. Pay when you leave. 5
Spend
Go Fukuoka lists average spends of about ¥1,500–2,500 a person. 8
Tipping
Not customary in Japan. Pay what the bill says. 12

At the counter5

  • Order at least one drink and one dish each.
  • Don't stay too long. Give up your seat when you've finished eating.
  • Come in a small group of two or three.
  • Leave big luggage in a locker or at your hotel.
  • There are no toilets at the stalls. Ask the staff where the nearest one is.
  • Most stalls don't do take-away, for hygiene reasons.
  • Each stall may post its own rules. When in doubt, ask the owner.

歴史A short history

  1. 869

    Gion Matsuri begins

    Kyoto's festival begins as a ritual against plague. Today its yoiyama evenings in mid-July fill central Kyoto with street stalls.16, 17

  2. 1603–1868

    Edo fast food

    Soba, sushi, tempura and eel are sold from stalls in Edo, some carried on a pole over the shoulder, some set up as fixed stands.14, 15

  3. 1770s

    Imagawayaki

    Round bean-paste cakes go on sale near Imagawa Bridge in Kanda, Edo.35

  4. 1909

    Taiyaki

    Naniwaya opens in Azabu-Jūban, Tokyo, and is credited with the fish-shaped version.34

  5. 1935

    Takoyaki

    Endo Tomekichi of Aizuya in Osaka is credited with putting octopus into the batter balls.20, 19

  6. 1955

    Nagahama

    The Nagahama fish market opens in Fukuoka. The stalls that fed its workers are said to have started kaedama.10

  7. 2013

    Fukuoka's yatai ordinance

    The city passes the Yatai Basic Ordinance, setting rules for where and how stalls operate.2

  8. 2016

    Open recruitment

    Fukuoka starts choosing new stall operators by public application and screening.3

  9. 2026

    Sixth round

    102 people apply for 23 stall sites.4

暦When to go

  • Jan 1–3

    Hatsumōde

    New Year shrine visits. Warm amazake is served at shrines.41

  • Early Feb

    Sapporo Snow Festival

    Usually 4–11 February, in Odori Park.18

  • Late Apr – mid Oct

    Sapporo corn carts

    Grilled corn and butter potatoes in Odori Park.28

  • Jul 15–16

    Gion Matsuri yoiyama

    Central Kyoto closes to cars from about 6pm to 11pm and the streets fill with stalls.17

  • Summer

    Festival season

    Shaved ice, candy apples and grilled corn.39, 37

  • Late autumn – winter

    Cold-weather stalls

    Stone-roasted sweet potatoes and oden.40, 33

  • Every evening

    Fukuoka

    About 100 stalls, weather permitting.1, 7
