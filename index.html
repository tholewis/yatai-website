屋台とはWhat a yatai is

The dictionary gives two meanings for 屋台. The first is a small shop with a roof (屋) over a stand (台), set up on a street or square. Some are on wheels and some are built into vehicles. The second is a roofed festival float that carries musicians or dancers. 1

This site is about the first kind: a counter, a roof, a lantern and usually one cook. You still find them in rows at festivals and shrine fairs. In Fukuoka, about 100 of them set up on the street every evening. 2

仕組みHow a stall works

A yatai is a kitchen, a dining room and a shopfront that all fit on a few square metres of pavement. Fukuoka sets out its rules in detail, so most of this section uses Fukuoka as the example.

  1. 1

    Roof 屋根

    The 屋 in 屋台. A small roof over the stand. 1

  2. 2

    Noren 暖簾

    A split cloth curtain across the front. In Fukuoka it goes up at dusk, when the stall opens. 18

  3. 3

    Lantern 提灯

    A paper lantern (chōchin) that lights the counter.

  4. 4

    Counter カウンター

    Customers sit at a counter with the cook on the other side. Most stalls seat around ten. 17

  5. 5

    Kitchen 厨房

    Everything is cooked inside the stall. At Fukuoka's stall sites the city has fitted a water supply, a drain and a power box that the operators plug into. 7

  6. 6

    Stools 椅子

    In Fukuoka, seats count toward the size limit. Stools, kitchen and storage must all fit within 3 m of frontage. 5

  7. 7

    Wheels 車輪

    Fukuoka's ordinance defines a yatai as a light vehicle (軽車両) fitted out as a restaurant. Each night it is packed up and taken away to storage. 4, 8

One night, start to finish

  1. 17:00

    Set up

    Set-up may not start before 5pm. The stall is wheeled out to its site. 12, 10

  2. about 18:30

    Open

    The noren goes up and the first customers sit down. 10, 18

  3. about 1:00

    Close

    Closing time varies from stall to stall. 10

  4. by 4:00

    Clear the street

    Everything must be off the road. Stalls are stored off the street, and transport vehicles may not be parked there. 12, 8

The rules in Fukuoka

Size
Up to 3 m wide along the pavement and 2.5 m deep. 5, 6
Pavement
At least 2 m of pavement must stay clear for people walking past. 2
Permits
A road occupancy permit from the ward office, plus a food business permit from the public health centre. Stalls in parks need a park permit. 9, 4
Who cooks
The permit holder must run the stall in person. Selling, subletting or lending the name of a stall is banned. 13, 14
Passing it on
A stall can pass only to a spouse or child. New operators are chosen by open application and screening. 14, 10

At the counter15

  • Order at least one drink and one dish each.
  • Give up your seat once you have eaten. Other people are waiting.
  • Come in twos and threes. Counters are small.
  • There are no toilets at the stall.
  • Pay when you leave. Most stalls take cash only.

Festival stalls

Festival stalls work differently. Traditionally they are run by tekiya, travelling stallholders whose networks go back to Edo-period peddlers and who historically allotted pitches at festivals. 19 Today a stall that cooks with gas, charcoal or electricity has to be registered with the local fire department before the event. Each one needs a fire extinguisher, and gas bottles and hoses must be secured. Rules were tightened after a generator fire at the 2013 Fukuchiyama fireworks. 20

歴史Four hundred years of stalls

From Edo's night soba carts to Fukuoka's licensed stalls.

  1. Edo period

    A city of single men

    Records from 1743 show men outnumbering women by about 1.5 to 1 in Edo, many of them workers living alone. Soba stalls fed them.23, 21

  2. 1686

    Fire is banned

    Fearing city fires, the shogunate banned fire at yatai. Night soba sellers, known as yotaka soba, carried on anyway.21

  3. 1700s

    Nihachi soba

    Soba sold as nihachi (二八). It may mean 2 parts wheat to 8 parts buckwheat, or a price of 2 × 8 = 16 mon. Sources give both.24, 21

  4. 1770s

    Imagawayaki

    Round bean-paste cakes go on sale near Imagawa Bridge in Kanda, Edo.43

  5. 1824

    From stall to shop

    Yohei-zushi goes from peddling, to a street stall, to a shop.22

  6. 1909

    Taiyaki

    Naniwaya in Azabu-Jūban is credited with the fish-shaped cake.42

  7. 1935

    Takoyaki

    Endo Tomekichi of Aizuya in Osaka is credited with putting octopus in the batter balls.30, 29

  8. 1945–

    The charumera

    Ramen stalls spring up in the postwar black markets. Vendors play the charumera, a small reed horn, to call customers.26, 25

  9. 1946

    Fukuoka's stalls begin

    Simple temporary shops appear on Fukuoka's roads in the confusion after the war.3

  10. 1947

    Tonkotsu

    The Sankyū stall in Kurume is credited with pork-bone broth. Later a Nagahama stall makes thin noodles and kaedama popular.26

  11. 1949–51

    Tokyo clears its streets

    GHQ orders street stalls off Tokyo's public roads. By 1951 Ginza's stalls are gone.27

  12. 1960s–70s

    Fukuoka's peak

    More than 400 stalls. Complaints about stalls on the road grow at the same time.3

  13. 1995

    One generation only

    With about 220 stalls left, police say no new operators will be allowed. Each stall is to last one generation.3

  14. 2013

    The ordinance

    Fukuoka passes the Yatai Basic Ordinance. It sets out rules for stall size, permits and who may run a stall, and opens the way for new operators.4, 14

  15. 2016

    New blood

    The city starts choosing new operators by open application and screening.10

  16. 2026

    Sixth round

    102 people apply for 23 stall sites. Fukuoka has about 100 stalls.11, 2

お品書きWhat the stalls cook

18 dishes you will find at yatai, grouped by how they are cooked.

鉄

On the iron griddle 鉄板

Cooked on hot iron plates and in iron moulds.

  • たこ焼き

    Takoyaki octopus balls

    Balls of batter with a piece of octopus inside, cooked in a griddle of half-round moulds. The sauce on top only arrived after the war.29

    Osaka

  • お好み焼き

    Okonomiyaki savoury pancake

    In Osaka, a batter of flour and dashi is mixed with cabbage and pork or squid, then cooked on both sides. In Hiroshima it is built in layers instead: a thin crêpe, cabbage, pork, noodles and egg.31, 32

    Osaka · Hiroshima

  • 焼きそば

    Yakisoba fried noodles

    Wheat noodles stir-fried on the plate with meat and vegetables in a Worcester-style sauce. One of the most common festival stalls.33, 34

    Festivals

  • いか焼き

    Ikayaki grilled squid

    Usually a whole squid, grilled and brushed with soy. In Osaka the name means chopped squid in a thin batter, pressed flat between two hot iron plates.35

    Festivals · Osaka

炭

Grill and fryer 炭火・揚げ

Skewers over the coals and anything that goes into hot oil.

  • 焼き鳥

    Yakitori grilled chicken

    Chicken on skewers, grilled and finished with tare (soy, sugar and mirin) or plain salt.36

    Everywhere

  • 焼きとうもろこし

    Yaki-tōmorokoshi grilled corn

    Corn grilled and brushed with soy sauce. In Sapporo it is called tōkibi and sold from carts in summer.37

    Summer

  • 唐揚げ

    Karaage fried chicken

    Marinated chicken, fried. The Japan Karaage Association traces it to Mikasa Kaikan in Ginza in 1932.38

    Festivals

  • フランクフルト

    Furanku sausage on a stick

    A thick sausage on a stick, grilled and finished with ketchup and mustard.39

    Festivals

鍋

From the pot 鍋

What a Fukuoka stall serves at night: broth, noodles and small plates to share.

  • 博多ラーメン

    Hakata ramen pork-bone ramen

    Tonkotsu broth with thin, straight noodles. Ask for kaedama to get a second serving of noodles in the same soup.28, 26

    Fukuoka

  • おでん

    Oden simmered pot

    Daikon, egg, konnyaku and more, kept simmering in broth. Fukuoka stalls add gyoza and beef tendon.40, 16

    Fukuoka · winter

  • 一口餃子

    Hitokuchi gyoza one-bite dumplings

    Bite-sized gyoza, a Hakata specialty.41

    Fukuoka

  • 明太玉子焼き

    Mentai tamagoyaki roe omelette

    A rolled omelette with spicy pollock roe (mentaiko), made for sharing across the counter.16

    Fukuoka

甘

Sweet 甘味

Moulded cakes, candied fruit and things on sticks.

  • たい焼き

    Taiyaki fish-shaped cake

    Batter baked in a fish-shaped mould around sweet bean paste.42

    Everywhere

  • 今川焼き

    Imagawayaki round bean cake

    The round original of taiyaki. Depending on the region it is also called ōbanyaki, kaitenyaki or oyaki.43

    Everywhere

  • みたらし団子

    Mitarashi dango glazed dumplings

    Grilled rice dumplings in a sweet soy glaze. Tradition links them to Shimogamo Shrine in Kyoto.44

    Kyoto

  • りんご飴

    Ringo-ame candy apple

    A whole apple in a shell of hard red candy.45

    Summer festivals

  • かき氷

    Kakigōri shaved ice

    Shaved ice with syrup. Sei Shōnagon mentions it in The Pillow Book, about a thousand years ago.46

    Summer

  • 焼き芋

    Yaki-imo stone-roasted sweet potato

    Sweet potato slow-roasted on hot stones and sold from trucks with a sung call.47

    Winter
